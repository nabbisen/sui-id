## §1: no list says less than it is

The rule, RFC 149 §5: **"every list states its count and its page. None silently truncates."**
- **A full list** (users, apps, the audit log) shows its **total**, and **which rows are on this page**, for example
  *"Showing 51–100 of 1,234"*. Previous and next are links that work without JavaScript.
- **An excerpt** (the dashboard's recent events, a user's recent events or sessions, `/me`'s recent events) says that it is
  the newest *N*, and links to the full list where one exists. Where none exists, it says the newest *N* are shown.
- **A count is the count of what it says.** The audit caption counts the matching entries, not the rows rendered.

## §2: paging

- **Users and apps**: 50 per page. The order is stable (the current `created_at ASC`, with the id as the tie-break). The page
  is in the query string (`?page=2`), and a page past the end shows the last page's links, not an error.
- **The audit log**: 50 per page, newest first, **paged by `seq`** (`?before=<seq>`), not by `OFFSET`. Two reasons:
  - new events arrive constantly, and `OFFSET` would shift every page under the reader;
  - a deep `OFFSET` scans every row before it.

  The filter is kept across pages.
- **The total**: one `COUNT(*)` with the same filter. Measure its cost on a table of 100,000 audit rows, and state the number
  in the return. If it costs more than the page itself by an order of magnitude, stop and return, with the measurement.
- **The audit CSV exports every matching row**, not the newest 2000:
  - streamed in pages by `seq`, so memory stays flat;
  - its filename or first line states the filter and the time of export;
  - **measure it at 100,000 rows**: the time, and the server's peak memory.

  If streaming cannot keep memory flat, stop and return. Do not cap it silently again.

## §3: the audit log reads better (RFC 149 §5)

- **Five columns**: time, actor, action (which wraps), target (truncated, with its copy button), and result.

  page does not.
- **The filter form fits at 375px with a 200% font.** No fixed `min-width` in `rem` may exceed the window: use
  `min(…, 100%)`, as Q3 did for the summary cards.

## §4: one name for one thing

Measured in Q4's review: the sidebar says **Apps**, while the app pages say *"Register a new client"* and *"Edit client"*.
RFC 074 renamed only the navigation label.
- The app pages' **headings, buttons and empty states** say *app*: *Add an app*, *Edit app*, *No apps yet*.
- **Protocol terms keep their names** where they are the protocol's: *client ID*, *client secret*, *confidential client*,
  *public client*. Each is a term a relying party's documentation uses.
- List every string you changed, in all three locales, in the return.
