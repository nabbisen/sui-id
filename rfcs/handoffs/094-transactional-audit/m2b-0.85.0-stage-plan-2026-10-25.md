## A — a correction to the scope document, before the stages

The scope document described wave 4 as *"the same shape, nine times"*:
nine `let _ = audit::append(...)` calls after a committed write. That is
true of the audit appends and it is **not the whole defect** for three of
the fourteen commands. Measured today in
`crates/sui-id-core/src/identity/admin/clients.rs`:

`set_client_disabled` (`:266-300`) and `delete_client` (`:303-331`) each
perform **two durable writes in sequence, neither in a transaction**, and
then a best-effort audit append:

```
set_client_disabled:  clients::set_disabled      → commits
                      refresh_tokens::revoke_all_for_client   ?        ← separate write
                      audit_with_note                                   ← best-effort
delete_client:        clients::soft_delete       → commits
                      caches.redirect_origins.rebuild
                      refresh_tokens::revoke_all_for_client   ?        ← separate write
                      audit_with_note                                   ← best-effort
```

The `?` on the revocation returns an error **after the client row has
already committed**, and before the audit append is reached. The
consequence is stated in the held dispatch rather than here; `0.85.0`
closes it, and the public record will carry it in the `CHANGELOG` once it
is closed rather than while it is open — `.github/SECURITY.md` reserves
that sequencing.

`contracts/write-commands.toml`'s C06 row already describes the mutation
surface as *"`clients::soft_delete` plus token revocation"*, so the
compound shape is not a new fact; what is new is that nothing makes the
two halves atomic, and that the ordering determines what an operator is
told.

**`crates/sui-id-store/src/repos/refresh_tokens.rs` has
`revoke_all_for_user_within_tx` (`:179`) but no client equivalent** —
`revoke_all_for_client` (`:191`) is `db`-taking only. Stage 1 therefore
builds the seam it needs rather than finding it.

## C — stages 2 and 3 re-measured, and the plan above was wrong

**Before dispatching stage 2 I measured its five commands and found the
split does not survive contact with the code.** §A called wave 4 *"the
same shape nine times"* and this plan assigned stages on that basis. Nine
is the number of `audit_ok`/`audit_with_note` **call sites**; it is not
the number of commands that have one. Counting the readers instead of
checking each description is the same error this project caught in wave 1
— where four of six "already atomic" writes turned out to emit nothing —
and I repeated it one wave later.

**Measured 2026-10-25 on `6e1e56d`**, every row checked individually:

| Shape | Commands | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Live write, best-effort append** — the assumed shape | C01, C02 (two sites), C03, C04, C07, K03 | `core clients.rs:112/206/256/138/165/374`, `signing_keys.rs:84` |
| **Live write, no audit record at all** | **C09**, **C08** | `http/handlers/admin/clients.rs:423`; `runtime/dev_mode.rs:516` |
| **No live write at all** | **C10**, **C22**, **K02** | no caller outside their repo modules |

`git grep` for the four event names confirms it from the other direction:
`client.consent_policy_changed`, `client.app_identity_changed`,
`client.registration_token.revoked` and `signing_key.retire` are **emitted
nowhere in `crates/`**.

**And one of them is a compound transition, like stage 1's.**
`clients_update_post` (`http/handlers/admin/clients.rs:415-426`) performs
**three durable writes in sequence**: `set_client_allowed_scopes` (C03),
`set_client_post_logout_redirect_uris` (C04), then a bare
`clients::update_consent_policy` (C09) called **straight from the handler,
bypassing the use-case layer, with no audit append anywhere**. One admin
form submission, three uncoordinated commits, two best-effort records and
one absent. A failure part-way leaves the client with its scopes replaced
and its post-logout URIs not — both security-relevant fields — and the
consent policy in whichever state the ordering left it.

## D — the revised stages

**Stage 2 — the client-update form (C03, C04, C09). LANDED** `f03eabb`,
Level B on that exact commit (`workflow_dispatch` `40184675744`, 27 jobs,
0 skipped). All three writes and their events commit in one transaction.

**G20 went 37 → 38, not 37 → 40, and that is the right answer.**
`Database::class_a` is generic over exactly one `CommandSpec` and appends
exactly one event per call, so three sealed commands cannot share a
transaction — the honest shape is **one** command, with C04 and C09 as
sibling inventory rows. The round measured the runner before designing,
which is what §2a of its dispatch asked for.

**It retires two emitted audit event names.** `client.set_allowed_scopes`
and `client.set_post_logout_redirect_uris` are emitted by nothing after
this stage; `client.update_form` replaces both, carrying the two counts
and the new consent policy. Nothing is lost informationally — but a name
is what a log query matches on, and these are published in
`docs/src/reference/audit-events.md`. **§G below requires `0.85.0`'s
summary to say so.**

**Stage 3 — the remaining live single writes (C01, C02, C07, K03, C08).
LANDED** `403bbc2`, Level B on that exact commit (`workflow_dispatch`
`40408445669`, 27 jobs, 0 skipped). **G20: 38 → 43**, as predicted. C08
took its own event name, `client.dev_secret_set`, because
`scripts/rust_registry.py` rejects two descriptors sharing one name —
the round measured that rather than taking the dispatch's lean on trust.

**It also fixed a defect older than this work.** Creating a client never
rebuilt the redirect-origins cache, though `http/cors.rs:100` asserts
the cache is rebuilt on every client mutation. A new client's origin
stayed absent until another mutation or a restart, so a browser app
wired to a fresh client was refused at preflight — fail-closed, so a
functional defect rather than an access one. **The fix has no test**:
removing it leaves all 600 e2e green. The test and a `CHANGELOG`
**Fixed** entry are required before the cut, §G.

**Stage 4 — the three dormant rows, and two things the cut needs first.
Dispatched 2026-10-27, and held in `.git-exclude/staging/`** for the same

Not a conversion stage — none of C10, C22 or K02 has a live write.

- **C22, registration-token revocation — wire it up.** The repo function

convert, and stage 2's three collapsed into one command. The work is
complete at 44 — the number was never the goal, and it is recorded here

## G — one required clause in `0.85.0`'s version summary

`CHANGELOG.md`'s `0.83.0` summary says, in the published record:

> This release does not close M3 — closure additionally needs a durable
> disposition of every candidate `sui-id admin legacy-dynamic-candidates`
> reports, and sign-off on that evidence …

**That loop is now closed and the public record does not say so.** M3
closed 2026-10-25. `0.85.0`'s version summary must carry a clause saying
it closed, and **on what basis** — that the disposition set was empty by
precondition, not because a report found zero. The distinction is the
whole of RFC 095's closure field and a reader who sees only "closed" will
assume the thing the RFC refuses.

**A second required clause: two audit event names are retired.**
`client.set_allowed_scopes` and `client.set_post_logout_redirect_uris`
are emitted by nothing after stage 2, replaced by `client.update_form`.
They are published in `docs/src/reference/audit-events.md`, so an
operator may be matching on them in a query, a filter or an alert, and
those stop matching silently. **This belongs in the summary, not buried
in a `Changed` paragraph about atomicity**, and the entry must name both
retired strings so a reader can grep their own tooling for them.

**A third: breaking `sui-id-core` API changes.** `delete_client` (stage
1) and `update_client_basic` (stage 3) gained a `clock` parameter;
`set_client_allowed_scopes` and `set_client_post_logout_redirect_uris`
(stage 2) and `update_client` (stage 3) were removed. Permitted on a
`0.x` minor bump, and still a break for anyone depending on the crate.

**A fourth, and this one is a `Fixed` entry rather than a summary
clause.** Creating a client never rebuilt the redirect-origins cache
that `http/cors.rs` consults, so a browser application wired to a
freshly created client was refused at CORS preflight until another
client mutation or a restart. Fixed in stage 3. **The fix is untested —
removing it leaves all 600 e2e green — so a test must land before the
cut**, with the entry. Stage 4 carries both if it lands first; otherwise
they land on their own. A user-facing fix shipping untested is not
acceptable, and the defect survived this long precisely because nothing
exercised it.

**Two bounds on the M3 clause's wording, and the preparation dispatch
will repeat them:**

- **Do not write that no deployment was affected.** What was established
  covers the deployments the closure assessment covers. Nothing in this
  project can see a third-party operator's database, and `0.76.3`–`0.79.0`
  were on crates.io.
- **Do not write "zero candidates"**, in those words or in substance. The
  command itself refuses to print a bare zero count; the `CHANGELOG`
  should not do what the tool was built not to do.

No standalone `CHANGELOG` entry: a milestone closure is not a change, and
M2a's closure on 2026-10-03 correctly left none. The loop was opened in a
summary and is closed in a summary.
