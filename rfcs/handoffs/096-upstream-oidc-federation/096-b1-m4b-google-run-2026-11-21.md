## 0 — safety, and what stays private

- **A throwaway instance only**: `../sui-id-live/`, its own database and
  key file, bound to `127.0.0.1:8801`. It is deleted afterwards, along with
  its Google OAuth client (§4).
- **The client secret never enters the chat, a log or the record.** You
  write it into a file yourself, and the start command reads that file into
  the environment variable sui-id takes it from (`client_secret_env`; sui-id
  has no other way to take one).
- **Who signs in at Google: you.** The architect types no password on a
  third party's page.
- **The record holds no personal data**: your Google email, the client ID
  and the user's name are redacted. Nothing else of yours is in it.

## 1 — your setup at Google: one page (Personal mode)

> Rewritten 2026-11-21, after `@nabbisen`: *"Google only ? The UI was far
> from easy to use."* and the choice *"Google, with my help"*. The path below
> is Google's **Personal mode**, which its help page describes as one flow
> that creates the project and the client together, for one Google account
> only. That is exactly what this test needs. Labels are quoted from Google's
> help pages (support.google.com/cloud, answers 15544987 and 15549257); a
> label those pages don't name is marked *(not confirmed)*.

1. Open **<https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/overview/personal>**,
   signed in as the Google account you will test with.
2. Application type: **Web application**.
3. Redirect URI: `http://localhost:8801/auth/federated/callback`. Paste it
   exactly: `localhost`, not `127.0.0.1`, and `http`, which Google allows
   for `localhost` only.
4. Accept the Terms of Service, and click **Continue**.
5. A dialog shows the **Client ID** and the **Client secret**, with
   **Download JSON**. **The secret is shown only this once**; afterwards
   Google keeps only its last four characters. Download the JSON, or keep
   the dialog open for the next step.
6. **Put the two values on your machine, not in the chat:**
   - the **client secret**, alone on one line, in
     `../sui-id-live/client-secret`. From this repository:
     `umask 077; $EDITOR ../sui-id-live/client-secret`;
   - the **Client ID**, in `../sui-id-live/sui-id.toml`, replacing
     `PASTE-CLIENT-ID-HERE`.

   Then delete the downloaded JSON.

No consent screen, no test users, no scopes to add. Google's audience
page says requests limited to `openid`, email and profile need neither,
and those are the only scopes sui-id asks for. Google notes that a new
client can take **"5 minutes to a few hours"** to take effect, so if
Google rejects the redirect at step 7, wait a few minutes and try again.

Then say **"Done"**.

**If the Personal-mode page does not open for your account:** say so, and
the architect gives you the longer path (a project, *Get started* with
*External*, yourself as a test user, then *Clients → Create client*).

## 2 — the configuration (the architect's)

`../sui-id-live/sui-id.toml`, from `--print-sample-config`, with:
- `issuer = "http://localhost:8801"`, `listen_addr = "127.0.0.1:8801"`,
  `cookie_secure = false`;
- `db_path` and `key_file` inside `../sui-id-live/`;
- this provider:

```toml
[[federation_providers]]
slug              = "google"
display_name      = "Google"
issuer            = "https://accounts.google.com"
client_id         = "PASTE-CLIENT-ID-HERE"
client_secret_env = "GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET"
scopes            = "openid email profile"
provision_mode    = "provision_on_first_login"
```

Then `sui-id preview-upgrade --config sui-id.toml`, which must report no
configuration error. The start command, in `../sui-id-live/`:

```sh
GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET="$(cat client-secret)" ../sui-id-stage14/target/release/sui-id --config sui-id.toml > server.log 2>&1
```

## 3 — the steps

Stage 14's twelve steps
([runbook §5](096-b1-stage14-live-run-runbook-2026-11-12.md)), with
*Google* for *Duende demo*, and your Google account where it says `alice`.
**You click**, including the sign-ins at Google. **The architect** reads
`server.log` and checks each step against the database. Steps 1–6 bring a
fresh instance to an enabled provider. **Steps 7–10 are what M4-B waits
for:**

| # | Do | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | On the login page, **Sign in with Google**, as your test account | back at sui-id, signed in as a **new** user, on that user's own security page (`/me/security`). The header shows only the account menu, none of the console's links |
| 8 | Sign in as the admin. **Users →** that user | a *Federated sign-in* section naming Google |
| 9 | **Unlink**, read the confirm page, confirm | the page says the unlink cannot be undone, and that open sessions stay valid until the account is disabled |
| 10 | Sign out, then **Sign in with Google** again | refused on the login page: "This sign-in could not be linked to a local account automatically. Contact an administrator." |

Steps 11 and 12 (disable, then delete) close the run, as in stage 14.

## 4 — afterwards

- **Delete the Personal-mode project** in the Google console (Personal
  mode names it `personal-mode-app` by default): *IAM & Admin → Settings →
  Shut down* *(not confirmed)*, or delete just its client. The architect
  cannot, and it holds a live secret.
- The architect stops sui-id, deletes `../sui-id-live/`, and moves the
  record into git as
  `096-b1-m4b-google-run-record-<date>.md`.
- If steps 7–10 pass, **M4-B closes** on your word, and RFC 097's draft
  starts. If not, the record says why, and the fix comes first.
