# RFC 096-B2 — amendment A4 was wrong, and the accepted design already answers it

> ## Whose turn it is: **yours**, for one decision (§3).
> **S1 is unaffected** and is being committed. **S2 (C23 at startup) does not
> start until you answer**, because A4 is what S2 builds.

**Prepared by.** The architect, 2026-11-11, while reviewing S1.

## 1 — what A4 said, and what the accepted design already says

**A4, as I wrote it and you accepted it** (RFC 096 §096-B2, amended 2026-11-11):
the trust fields are `issuer`, `client_id`, the client secret, `scopes` and
`provision_mode`. A change disables the provider. `display_name` does not.
And **`allowed_origins` keeps RFC 134's ruling (C24, no disable).**

**RFC 096 already defines the trust policy, precisely**, in its policy digest
(`rfcs/accepted/096-upstream-oidc-federation-validation.md:424-435`). The
digest is SHA-256 over the following, in order:
- issuer;
- client ID;
- the sealed client secret's hash;
- the **token-auth method**;
- scopes;
- the **ID-token algorithms**;
- the **endpoint origins**;
- the **HTTPS ports**;
- provision mode.

*"Enabled/review status, both counters, evidence, and display labels are
excluded because they are bound separately or are not trust policy."*

**So A4 was wrong in two ways, and both are the architect's error:**
1. **It was incomplete.** It left out four of the nine fields: auth method,
   ID-token algorithms, endpoint origins and ports. A4 should have pointed at
   this definition instead of writing its own list. I wrote the list from the
   fields `startup.rs` happens to warn about, which measured the code instead of
   the design.
2. **Its `allowed_origins` carve-out contradicts the digest.** C17's enable
   evidence is bound to the digest (RFC 094 `:506-512`). An origin change under
   C24, with no disable, would leave an **enabled** provider whose stored
   evidence no longer matches its own policy. Widening the origin set is also a
   **trust expansion**: it lets discovery point at a host the last preflight
   never checked.

## 2 — why the carve-out isn't needed

RFC 134 needed a **non-destructive** way to change origins, because
delete-and-recreate cascades and destroys every federation link
(`rfcs/done/134-federation-egress-is-a-policy.md:130-142`). In October, C24
(write the change, don't disable) was the only path. **C23 is non-destructive
too**: it disables and keeps links. So routing an origin change through C23
meets RFC 134's requirement *and* RFC 096's digest. The operator re-checks and
re-enables in one step, and no link is lost.

## 3 — what is yours

**Replace A4 with the accepted design's own definition.**
- **Trust policy is exactly the digest's fields** (RFC 096 `:424-435`). A change
  to any of them goes through C23: the provider is disabled until re-checked and
  re-enabled.
- **That includes `allowed_origins`, and C24 folds into C23.** One path for
  every trust-policy change, enabled or not, is simpler, and leaves nothing to
  misread. For a disabled provider, C23 increments the counters and the
  provider stays disabled, which is correct. C23's event already records the
  changed fields, origins among them (RFC 094 `:563-567`). So
  `federation.provider.allowed_origins_changed` stops being written, and C24's
  contract row is retired, like C19's.
- **`display_name`** stays outside, as the RFC already says.

**Recommended.** The alternative is to keep A4 as accepted, which means that
on an enabled provider an origin change leaves the evidence bound to a digest
the provider no longer has. I can't recommend that, but it is your call.

Either way, RFC 096's amendment text is corrected to say which, and S2 builds
that.
