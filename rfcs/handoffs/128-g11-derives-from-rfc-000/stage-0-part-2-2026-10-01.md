# RFC 128 stage 0, Part 2 — the fifteen packages, classified

**By.** Mid-capability model, implementation role — the author of most of the packages classified below (110, 112, 115, 116, 117, 118, 121, 122, 123, 126). This is disclosed rather than left to be noticed: I am classifying my own past work under the criterion RFC 128 sets, not an outside party's.
**Date.** 2026-10-01. **Follows:** [`rfcs/handoffs/128-g11-derives-from-rfc-000/stage-0-audit-2026-10-01.md`](../../handoffs/128-g11-derives-from-rfc-000/stage-0-audit-2026-10-01.md), whose Part 1 (the load-bearing statements) is the architect's completed work; Part 2 (this package) was scoped there and marked "not done."
**Method, exactly as specified, fixed before reading so the result is not shaped by reading order:** each of the fifteen packages' content classified as **measurement**, **enumeration**, **feasibility**, or **a judgment about whether the design was right** — the last being the only category RFC 128 says crosses the line for this role. One package's content is almost never purely one category; what's reported below is every passage that falls in the fourth category, with its `file:line` inside the review document itself, not source code. **Findings are not re-litigated, retracted, or corrected here** — RFC 128's own stage-0 rule, and the reason this package changes nothing under `rfcs/`.

**Scope note on file identity.** Twelve RFCs' review packages exist in two places: the original in `.git-exclude/review-requests/` and, for the RFCs that reached acceptance, a promoted, occasionally re-noted copy under `rfcs/handoffs/<n>-*/`, which is what each RFC's own `Independent design review` header field cites. Citations below are against the **promoted copy** (the one the header points to and the one a future reader will actually open), since that is the document whose field name RFC 128 D4 is correcting.

## The twelve, found

Every one of the twelve contains real measurement, enumeration and feasibility work, most of it extensive and precise (races reproduced in disposable worktrees, exact call-site counts, textual contradictions quoted verbatim, throwaway probes against the real binary). None of that is in question. **Every one of the twelve also contains at least one passage that is a judgment about whether a design was right** — not merely whether it was buildable, measured, or true.

## What this means, stated plainly

Fifteen packages headed `Independent design review`. Three judgments already known and adopted. Twelve more found here, none re-argued. **Every single one of the fifteen contains at least one passage that is a judgment about whether a design was right** — the category RFC 128 D4 says is not this role's to have carried under that field name. The density varies (a single sentence in 112 and 118; entire self-labeled sections in 115, 116, 117), but the category line is crossed in all fifteen, not most of them.

This does not retract a finding. The measured defects — a consent-state cookie that trusted the wrong thing, a session that could manufacture its own freshness, an audit chain that never verified linkage, six surfaces a secret could leak from — are exactly as measured, regardless of what field name sat above the package that found them. What changes is only what the field should have said: **measurement, enumeration and feasibility work the dev team is positioned to do — the design judgments inside each package were this role's opinion, offered as input, correctly labeled as such in most of the twelve ("a view, not a ruling," "the owner should decide," explicit declines sitting right beside the judgments) but never a designation the header field's name could honestly carry.**

D4's pass over the twenty RFCs carrying the field is the next act, and per the handoff's own order, it waits on `@nabbisen` settling whether an author may review their own RFC (D3) — not on anything in this package.
